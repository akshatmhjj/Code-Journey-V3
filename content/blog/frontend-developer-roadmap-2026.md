Frontend is still one of the most popular ways into software engineering, and still one of the most crowded. The good news: the path is well understood. The catch: AI now produces first drafts of components in seconds, so employers expect more judgement from juniors than they used to.

This roadmap follows the [Frontend Engineer route](/roles/frontend-engineer). Each skill links to what to learn and the best free resources, official docs first.

## The route, in order

At around 10 hours a week, plan on **7–10 months** to job-ready.

### 1. Foundations (10–14 weeks)

- [How the internet works](/skills/how-the-internet-works) — requests, DNS, what a browser actually does.
- [HTML](/skills/html) and [CSS](/skills/css) — properly, including layout with Flexbox and Grid. Don't rush this.
- [JavaScript](/skills/javascript) — the language itself before any framework: functions, objects, async, the DOM.
- [Git](/skills/git) and the [command line](/skills/command-line).

**Project:** a responsive personal site with a contact form, published on your own URL.

### 2. Core (12–16 weeks)

- [TypeScript](/skills/typescript) — most frontend job posts now expect it.
- [React](/skills/react) — still the most-used web framework in the Stack Overflow Developer Survey.
- [REST APIs](/skills/rest-apis) and [state management](/skills/state-management) — loading, error and empty states are where real apps get hard.
- [Accessibility](/skills/accessibility) — keyboard use, labels, contrast. Increasingly a legal requirement, and a quick way to stand out.

**Project:** a job board that loads listings from a public API, with search, filters, loading and error states — fully usable with only a keyboard.

### 3. Job-ready (8–12 weeks)

- [Next.js](/skills/nextjs) and [Tailwind CSS](/skills/tailwind-css) — the stack many teams have settled on.
- [Frontend testing](/skills/frontend-testing) and [web performance](/skills/web-performance).
- [Deployment](/skills/deployment), so your work lives at a real URL.
- [AI coding tools](/skills/ai-coding-tools) — use them daily, and learn to review what they produce.

**Project:** a full app with sign-in, a database-backed feature, automated tests in CI, and a Lighthouse mobile score above 90.

## What you can skip (for now)

Learning three frameworks. Memorising every CSS property. A long DSA grind before you can build anything — [we've written about that](/blog/dsa-myth). Go deep on one stack and ship things with it.

## What it pays

On PayScale India, front end developers report an average base salary of about **₹6.6 L a year**, with most between ₹2.9 L and ₹20 L, and around ₹3.6 L in the first year (872 reports). In the US, software developers — the closest official category — have a median of about $136k, with jobs projected to grow 10% from 2025 to 2035, against 3.5% for all jobs.

The spread is wide. The top of it goes to people who can own a feature end to end — which is why many frontend developers grow into [full-stack](/roles/compare/frontend-engineer-vs-full-stack-engineer) or [design engineering](/roles/design-engineer). See [pay for every role](/market).

## How AI is changing it

AI assistants draft components, styles and tests quickly, so the entry bar has moved up. What stays valuable is judgement: knowing whether generated UI is accessible, fast and maintainable, structuring state so features don't collide, and turning vague requirements into clear interfaces. Treat AI as a fast junior pair — useful, and always reviewed.
